What is GEO

GEO stands for Generative Engine Optimization and it is the practice of preparing your website pages for AI assistants like ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews. GEO is important today as never before because AI models read content online and treat it as sources to answer questions of your potential clients.

In early 2026, 68% of Google searches ended without a single click, and when Google shows an AI summary, people click a regular result on only 8% of visits. The answer itself became the battleground. Your page either gets quoted inside it or stays invisible.

Classic SEO no longer guarantees this. Ahrefs says that only 38% of pages cited in AI Overviews also rank in the top 10 for the same query. GEO is a separate discipline with its own rules. Research from Princeton confirms that applying those rules can lift a page's AI visibility by up to 40%.

What is GEO checker

The GEO checker is a free tool that measures how ready one page is to be cited by AI engines. You paste a URL and get a report in a few seconds. No signup, no email gate. The tool reads your page the way AI systems do, then runs 52 individual signals across 7 categories: content structure, machine-readable markup, evidence quality, AI crawler access, content humanness, linguistical patterns, and freshness.

Each signal returns numbers you can work with. Short comments explaining what each finding means are provided at the end of each run.

How to read GEO scores

The total score runs from 0 to 100 and maps to six grades. 90 or above earns an A+, meaning the page is genuinely well prepared for AI citation. 80 to 89 is an A, strong with minor gaps. 70 to 79 is a B, solid but with clear room to improve. 55 to 69 is a C, meaning AI engines can read the page but better-prepared competitors will usually be picked first. 40 to 54 is a D, and anything below 40 is an F, largely invisible to AI search.

Treat the score as a readiness measure and never as guarantee that the page will be cited by AI. No tool can promise you that because AI engines weigh many factors including your overall domain authority. What the score tells you reliably is whether your page helps or hurts your chances, and exactly where.

What each category shows

The 100 points are split across seven categories, which are as follows:

  • Page Architecture (23 points) checks whether your content is structured so AI can lift answers from it: headings, question-phrased subheadings, short sections, lists and FAQ blocks.
  • Machine Readability (15 points) inspects your structured data markup and image descriptions.
  • Trust Signals (15 points) counts statistics, source citations, expert quotes and author identity which are all very important to AI models.
  • AI Crawler Access (15 points) tests whether the 11 major AI crawlers are allowed in, whether your text is readable without JavaScript, and whether an llms.txt file exists.
  • Human Voice (15 points) runs 15 detectors for typical AI-writing patterns that make content sound generic.
  • Linguistic Signature (12 points) measures sentence variety, vocabulary breadth and paragraph sizing.
  • Freshness (5 points) checks visible and structured update dates.

How GEO score is calculated

Every one of the 52 signals is a concrete measurement taken from your live page: counts, rates, presence checks. Each signal earns points inside its category, category points add up to the total, and thresholds are fixed in code. No AI model judges your content, which is why results are consistent and reliable.

The category weights follow published evidence. Statistics, quotations and source citations each lifted AI visibility by 30 to 40% in the Princeton GEO benchmark, so Trust Signals carries heavy weight. AI assistants cite content that is 25.7% more recent than organic results on average, so freshness earns dedicated points.

AI engines do not publish their algorithms so nobody knows exactly the scoring criteria. Our weights reflect the best available research (Princeton, Ahrefs, etc.) plus our own testing, and we update them as new studies come out.

Common questions

What does the GEO checker actually measure?

It measures how ready one specific page is to be found and quoted by AI engines like ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews. The score combines 52 measurable signals across 7 categories, from content structure to crawler access to how human the writing sounds.

How is it different from the SEO checker?

The goal is different. An SEO checker measures how well a page can rank in a list of links, so it looks at keywords, meta tags, backlinks and technical health. GEO checker measures how well a page can be quoted inside an AI answer. AI engines lift short passages, and they favor direct answers, verified facts with named sources, machine-readable structure, and writing that sounds human. Most of those signals never appear in a classic SEO audit.

Is the tool really free?

Yes. No signup, no email, no trial. To keep the tool fast for everyone, each visitor can run up to 30 scans per hour and 100 per day.

How long does a scan take?

Usually 2 to 5 seconds. If you scan the same URL again within 24 hours, you will see the same report instantly.

Which URL should I test?

Test a real content page: a blog post, a guide, a product explainer. That is what AI engines cite. Homepages usually score lower because they are navigation hubs with little liftable content, and that is expected.

Can a high score guarantee that AI will cite my page?

No, and be careful with any tool that promises this. AI engines also weigh factors outside a single page, such as your domain's overall authority and how often your brand appears in their training data. What the score shows reliably is whether your page helps or blocks your chances, and which specific fixes would improve them.

My page ranks well in Google. Why is my GEO score low?

Because the two systems reward different things. Ahrefs says that only 38% of pages cited in Google's AI Overviews also rank in the organic top 10. A page can rank through backlinks and domain strength while still lacking the structure, evidence density and crawler access that AI citation requires.

Why did my score change since last week?

The checks are deterministic, so the same page content always scores the same. If the score moved, something changed: the page itself, its robots.txt, its structured data, or its update dates. Freshness checks also shift naturally as content ages.

What are the Human Voice and Linguistic Signature categories?

They analyze how the page is written. Human Voice runs 15 detectors for patterns typical of unedited AI text, like filler phrases, unsourced claims and hype vocabulary. Linguistic Signature measures the statistical shape of your prose: sentence variety, vocabulary breadth, paragraph sizing. Content that sounds human earns reader trust, and 62% of consumers say they engage less with content they know is AI-made.

Does using AI to write content hurt my score?

Not by itself. The tool does not detect whether AI was involved. It detects whether the text shows the patterns of unedited machine writing. Well-edited AI-assisted content passes these checks. Mass-produced, generic content does not, and Google's own policies treat that kind of content as spam.

What is llms.txt and do I need it?

llms.txt is an emerging standard: a plain-text file on your site that gives AI systems a curated map of your best content. Adoption is early and no major AI vendor has confirmed using it yet, so it carries only 2 points in our scoring. It is cheap to add and positions you well if adoption grows.

My site is behind bot protection. Can you still check it?

Often yes. The tool has a fallback for pages that block simple automated visits, and the report will tell you honestly when parts of the page could not be verified. If nothing can be read at all, you get a clear message instead of an inaccurate score. That result is itself useful because if we cannot read your page, many AI crawlers cannot either. And this can hurt your AI visibility.

Do you store my page or my results?

We keep the scored report in a short-lived cache for up to 24 hours so repeat scans return instantly. We do not publish your results or share them with third parties.

How often should I re-check a page?

After every meaningful edit, and otherwise about once a month. AI search is moving fast, freshness decays on its own, and we update the checks as new research appears, so periodic re-scans keep your picture current.

My score is low. What should I fix first?

Open the categories with the most missed points and start there. Crawler access issues come first, because if AI bots cannot read the page, nothing else matters. Structure and evidence fixes usually move the score most after that.
